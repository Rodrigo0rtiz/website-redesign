Growing Guide
Practical, actionable recommendations for Washington State apple farmers
💧
Irrigation Management
Drip irrigation delivers the best profitability — dataset shows drip farms earn $152,660 avg profit vs -$160,360 for overhead. That's a $313k difference per farm.
Target soil moisture 55–75% through fruit development. Peak demand during cell division (June) and sizing (July–Aug) — never deficit irrigate these stages.
Apply 350–600 acre-feet per season depending on variety and summer heat. Columbia Basin farms typically need the most supplemental water.
Reduce irrigation 2–3 weeks before harvest to improve fruit firmness and color. Irregular wetting causes bitter pit in Honeycrisp and cracking in Fuji.
🌿
Soil & Nutrient Management
Soil quality directly drives yield — dataset shows soil score 10 averages 45,898 lbs/acre vs 32,158 for score 2. Every point improvement adds ~1,500 lbs/acre.
Test soil pH every 2–3 years. Target 6.0–7.0 for optimal nutrient availability in Eastern WA sandy loam soils.
Nitrogen in split doses: 40% at bloom, 30% at petal fall, 30% in June. Excess N delays color and shortens storage life.
Honeycrisp is especially calcium-sensitive. Apply foliar calcium every 10–14 days from petal fall through mid-July to prevent bitter pit.
✂️
Pruning & Canopy
Prune during dormancy (Jan–Mar) before bud break. Remove ~20–25% of canopy annually for light penetration and airflow.
High-density plantings (300+ trees/acre) require annual summer pruning to control canopy and improve fruit color.
Thin fruit to one per cluster by 30 days after full bloom. Proper thinning is the single biggest factor in fruit size and return bloom.
Honeycrisp is highly sensitive to over-cropping — thin aggressively to 6–8" spacing. Under-thinning causes small fruit and biennial bearing.
🐛
Pest & Disease Management
Codling moth: use pheromone traps from tight cluster through harvest. WSU Decision Aid System (DAS) provides degree-day spray timing — the standard tool in WA.
Apple scab is the top disease threat during wet springs. Apply protective fungicides from green tip through petal fall.
Fire blight is critical for young orchards. Copper at silver tip; Apogee during active shoot growth; avoid excess nitrogen in fire blight-prone blocks.
Organic operations: incorporate kaolin clay, copper, and biocontrol agents as part of an integrated pest management (IPM) program.
📅
Harvest Planning
Monitor starch-iodine index, firmness, and Brix together — no single test alone is reliable. WSU Extension provides target ranges by variety.
Gala: late July–August. Honeycrisp: September. Cosmic Crisp / Fuji / Granny Smith: October–November.
Plan 2–3 harvest passes over 2–3 weeks for large blocks to optimize fruit maturity and size distribution.
Book harvest labor and CA storage space by August 1 — labor shortages in Eastern WA during peak season (Sept–Oct) are common.
🏪
Storage & Post-Harvest
CA storage (1–2% O₂, 1–2% CO₂, 32°F) is essential for Fuji, Granny Smith, and Cosmic Crisp — extends marketability to 8–12 months.
Honeycrisp requires specialized CA at 38°F — lower temperatures cause chilling injury. Do not store with standard CA parameters.
Pre-cool fruit within 2–4 hours of harvest. Delays in pre-cooling reduce shelf life significantly, especially for Honeycrisp.
Farms with CA storage access earn 20–30% higher returns through off-season market timing — critical for Honeycrisp and Cosmic Crisp.
Risk & Alert System
Current season risk indicators — based on wa_apple_farm_synthetic_v2 dataset patterns

Drip Irrigation — STRONG
681 of 1,200 farms (56.8%) use drip, achieving avg 40,467 lbs/acre and $152k avg profit. Highest performing irrigation type in dataset by a wide margin.
✅
Honeycrisp Profitability — HIGH
229 Honeycrisp farms average $804,582 profit per farm — the strongest returns in the entire dataset. Only 21 flagged with drought risk.
✅
Cosmic Crisp Outlook — POSITIVE
110 Cosmic Crisp farms average $509,564 profit and 41,729 lbs/acre — highest yield AND profitable. Only 5 drought risk farms flagged.
⚠️
Drought Risk — 76 FARMS
6.3% of all farms carry a drought risk flag. Honeycrisp has the most (21) due to high water demand. Adams and Franklin counties face most rainfall variability.
⚠️
Overhead Irrigation — UNDERPERFORMING
133 overhead farms average a $160,360 loss per farm — the worst of any irrigation type. Switching to drip or micro-sprinkler should be evaluated immediately.
⚠️
Small Farm Profitability Gap
628 farms under 5 acres average a $21,014 loss per farm and 35,573 lbs/acre — significantly below the 42,000–44,000 range of larger operations.
🚨
Gala Profitability Crisis — ACTION NEEDED
308 Gala farms average a loss of $132,008 per farm despite $365.8M total revenue. High equipment costs erode margins. The most-planted variety is losing money on average.
🚨
Red Delicious & Granny Smith Losses
Red Delicious: avg -$285,621/farm. Granny Smith: avg -$318,166/farm. Evaluate all blocks of these varieties for replanting to Honeycrisp or Cosmic Crisp.
🚨
No-Irrigation Farms — YIELD RISK
74 farms with no irrigation average only 28,868 lbs/acre — 26% below dataset average. Without water access, yield potential is severely limited in Eastern WA.
